* Custom Symbols

** STATUS -- steps 0 to 6 decided 2026-11-20 (user decision), building
Written 2026-11-19 as a proposal. § OPEN QUESTIONS 1 to 6 are decided, and
steps 0 to 6 are being built for 0.3.0, on the =custom-symbols= branch. Step
0 is built (2026-11-20). Step 1 is built (2026-11-21), with the atlas's
padding and mipmaps, its premultiplied pixels and its limits decided for
it. Step 2 is built (2026-11-21) but for SVG markup, which waits on a
question (§ SVG output): markers draw, pick and write SVG from pixels,
natively and in Chrome's WebGL2, and the spike's views without markers are
byte-identical to step 1's. Step 7, vector symbols, waits on
§ OPEN QUESTIONS 7 and is a release of its own. § STEPS builds one kind of
symbol at a time. Markers, dashes and pattern fills are three parts that
share an atlas and a palette layout, so each can be built once the shared
step is in.

** THE PROBLEM
A style class draws one of three looks (plans/rendering.org § STYLING): a
flat fill, a solid line with round caps and joins, and a point as a round
dot. Maps need more than that:

- *Markers.* A point drawn as an image: a school, a well, a station, a
  north-pointing arrow. Chosen by class, so an embedder's own logic picks
  which marker each feature gets, as it picks colours now.
- *Dashed and dotted lines.* Boundaries, proposed routes, trails, ferry
  lines. Dashed polygon outlines too.
- *Pattern fills.* Hatching, stipple and texture for wetlands, protected
  areas and uncertain extents. Also how a map stays readable in one colour,
  on paper or for readers who can't tell colours apart.

All three need to look the same on screen, in a pick and in SVG output, as
everything else nazca draws does.

** WHAT EXISTS
Measured against =src/= at 0.2.1:

- *=Style= has four fields*: =fill=, =line=, =line_width= and =point_size=,
  all plain values. A =Palette= is 256 of them, uploaded as a 256 x 3
  =RGBA32F= texture: a fill row, a line row, and a row holding the width and
  point size (=palette_texels=). =update_palette= rewrites it in one upload.
- *Meshes don't depend on styles.* A vertex carries its position, its class
  and its feature id; a class's look is read from the palette at draw time.
  So restyling never re-prepares a tile, and a mesh can be prepared off the
  thread that draws it.
- *Points are lines of no length.* =LINE_VS= draws an instanced quad per
  segment and =LINE_FS= keeps what lies within half the width, with a
  one-pixel antialiased edge. Under =u_points= it draws =Mesh::points= the
  same way, with the point size as the width and the fill row as the
  colour: a round dot.
- *Lines are segments that don't know their neighbours.* Each one is an
  instance (=SEGMENT_BYTES= = 24: both ends, the class, the id) with round
  ends, so joins are round. No segment knows how far along its line it
  is.
- *Fills read one texel.* =FILL_FS= takes the class's fill colour from the
  palette. A fill that draws nothing still picks.
- *Each tile draws only in its own pixels.* The stencil claim (=gl.rs='s
  module docs) cuts each tile's draw at its edge, grown by one pixel.
  Anything that reaches over the edge has to be drawn again by the
  neighbour, from the neighbour's own geometry in its guard band
  (=Detail::guard_px=, 32 by default). =MeshBuilder::point= keeps a point
  only inside the tile plus that guard band.
- *Programs come in pairs under defines.* Each draw program is compiled
  four ways (screen, =STATE=, =PICK=, =PICK= with =STATE=) from one source.
- *SVG writes one =<path>= per class for fills and one for lines* in each
  layer, so tile splits leave no seams. Points are =<circle>=s. Styles are
  CSS classes. Nothing clips one tile's output to its own area.
- *What nazca doesn't do* (project.org): it reads no files, and it holds
  geometry and an id per feature, never attributes. Symbols arrive from
  the embedder as bytes, and anything that varies per feature arrives as a
  class.

** DESIGN

*** The shared part: symbols, an atlas, and a wider palette
- *A =symbol= module, standard-library only.* A =Symbol= is an image the
  embedder hands in: RGBA8 pixels, its scale, an anchor (the point of the
  image that sits on the feature, as fractions of its width and height,
  the centre by default), and its kind:
  - /Colour/: drawn as it is. The alpha of the class's =fill= colour
    multiplies its alpha, so fading a class's fill fades its markers too.
  - /Mask/: only its alpha is used, tinted by a colour from the class. One
    hatch or one pin serves every colour a palette gives it.
  It may also carry SVG markup, which only the SVG writer reads
  (§ SVG output).
- *=Symbols= holds them by id.* =Symbols::add= returns a =SymbolId= (a
  =u16=) and packs the image into an atlas on the CPU, in shelves with a
  padding around each image. The atlas is plain bytes and a rectangle per
  id, so the SVG writer and the GPU agree on ids without a GPU in the room.
  An id is never reused within a =Symbols=; removing a symbol frees its
  space at the next repack.
- *Ids and the atlas have limits.* Decided 2026-11-21 (user decision).
  - /The atlas is at most 2048 x 2048/, the largest texture WebGL2
    promises. An image that can't fit is an error naming it. Space, not
    ids, bounds the symbols held at once: with their padding, 784 images of
    64 pixels or 2,601 of 32.
  - /Ids run out after 65,536 adds to one =Symbols=/: every =add= takes
    one, a symbol added again at another size included, and =remove=
    gives none back, so a stale palette can't draw whatever took a removed
    symbol's place. The limit is nazca's, from the =u16=; the embedder
    reaches it only by adding and removing over a long session (50 markers
    added again at each of 40 zoom thresholds take 2,000), and starts a new
    =Symbols= and writes its palettes again. Vector symbols (step 7) stay
    sharp at any size and need no adding again.
  Ruled out: reusing an id once the embedder says no palette names it, an
  API to call correctly for a limit not likely met; =u32= ids, which no
  table of 256 x 256 covers.
- *The images are larger than they are drawn.* The embedder rasterizes at
  the device's pixel ratio, or more for a symbol it will draw at several
  sizes, and gives that as the symbol's scale: image pixels per drawn
  pixel, in the pixels widths are already in. A symbol has no size of its
  own; the style gives it one (=point_size= for a marker, the repeat for a
  pattern). The GPU samples with linear filtering and mipmaps, so a symbol
  drawn smaller stays smooth. Drawn larger than its pixels divided by its
  scale, it blurs: the embedder adds it again at a larger size, and a
  palette can be asked which classes draw a symbol past that size
  (=Palette::blurred=), as =Symbol::facets= names faceted curves.
- *Mipmaps stop at a quarter.* Decided 2026-11-21 (user decision). The
  mipmaps are built from the whole atlas, so a level whose texels average
  across two symbols' slots mixes one symbol's pixels into the other's
  edge: a faint rim of a neighbour's colour on a marker drawn small.
  Clamping the lookup to a symbol's rectangle doesn't prevent it, since the
  texel inside the rectangle already holds the neighbour. So each image has
  4 transparent pixels of padding, each slot starts on a multiple of 4, and
  the texture stops at level 2 (=TEXTURE_MAX_LEVEL=): a level-2 texel is an
  aligned 4 x 4 block, inside one slot or in padding, never across two. A
  symbol drawn at a quarter of its pixels divided by its scale or larger is
  smooth; smaller, it samples level 2 sparsely and can shimmer, and the
  embedder adds it again at a smaller size, as it does for one drawn too
  large. Padding of 8 and level 3 would hold to an eighth, for slots of
  about 56% more area than the image (a 64-pixel image in an 80-pixel
  slot) where 4 costs about 27% (72). Only markers drawn small meet the
  floor, as on a zoom curve zoomed out; print draws symbols large.
- *The atlas is premultiplied.* Decided 2026-11-21 (user decision). A
  symbol arrives with straight alpha, as PNGs, a canvas's =getImageData=
  and =Style='s colours have it, and its transparent pixels are usually
  black. Filtering averages each channel on its own, at every mipmap and
  between texels at full size, so a white edge beside a transparent pixel
  averages to (0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5): grey at half opacity, a dark rim
  around a white icon. Premultiplied, the same texel is white at half
  opacity. So =Symbols= multiplies each pixel's colour by its alpha as it
  packs the atlas, and the marker and pattern shaders divide by alpha after
  sampling, so blending stays as it is (straight =SRC_ALPHA=). A =Symbol=
  keeps the embedder's bytes, which a repack needs anyway, and the SVG
  writer's PNGs are written from them, exact. Premultiplying in 8 bits
  coarsens the colour of nearly transparent pixels (at alpha 3/255 a
  channel keeps 4 levels), which is not seen. A mask uses only its alpha
  and is unaffected. Ruled out: filling transparent pixels with the nearest
  visible colour, which fails where two colours meet a transparent area,
  and asking the embedder for premultiplied pixels, which a canvas doesn't
  give.
- *=Style= gains three optional fields*:
  - =marker: Option<Marker>=: a =SymbolId=, a rotation in degrees, and
    whether that rotation is against the map (turns as the camera turns)
    or against the screen. Its size is =point_size=, which now means the
    marker's longest side. A class with a marker draws it in place of the
    dot. A mask marker takes the =fill= colour.
  - =dash: Option<Dash>=: up to eight on and off lengths, how many of them
    are used, and a phase. It applies to the class's lines and polygon
    outlines. The count is stored, not inferred from zeros: an /on/ length
    of 0 is a dot (§ Dashes), so 0 can't also mark a length unused.
  - =pattern: Option<Pattern>=: a =SymbolId= and the size of one repeat. It
    takes the place of the flat fill. A mask pattern takes the =fill= colour,
    so a hatch over a background is two draws of the mesh with two palettes,
    as two looks of one layer already are.
  =Style= stays =Copy= and its =Default= draws nothing. It and the three
  new types become =#[non_exhaustive]= (§ OPEN QUESTIONS 6): an embedder
  starts from =Style::default()=, =Marker::new(id)=, =Dash::new(lengths,
  phase)= or =Pattern::new(id, repeat)= and assigns the fields it wants, so
  a field added later breaks no one. =Dash::new= takes a slice and refuses
  more than eight lengths or a negative one, so its count and its lengths
  can't disagree.
- *The palette texture grows rows*, from 256 x 3 to 256 x 7 (the proposal
  said 6, miscounting these four new rows): a marker row
  (id, rotation, alignment), two dash rows (eight lengths), and a pattern
  row (id, repeat size, and the dash phase and dash count in its last two
  channels). Restyling is still one upload; a class's marker or dash
  changes with no mesh touched. An id is written as id + 1, so 0 is no
  marker or no pattern and a class left at =Style::default()= is all zeros
  and draws nothing, as now; an =f32= holds every =u16= exactly.
- *=GpuSymbols= is the atlas on the GPU*: an =RGBA8= texture with mipmaps,
  and an =RGBA32F= table of each id's rectangle in it. Adding a symbol
  re-uploads both. A palette names symbols by id, not by rectangle, so a
  repack touches no palette and no mesh. The table holds 256 ids a row, id
  /n/ in row /n/ / 256, as two texels: its rectangle, then its anchor and
  kind, which the marker shader needs as well. So it is 512 wide and only
  as tall as the highest id's row: a row of 8 KB for a few hundred ids,
  2 MB at most, and never past WebGL2's texture limit as a row of 65,536
  would be.
- *The renderer holds the atlas.* Decided 2026-11-21 (user decision).
  =Renderer::set_symbols(gl, &Symbols)= uploads it, after the embedder adds
  or removes symbols, and every draw and pick uses it with no argument of
  its own; a renderer starts with an empty atlas, so an embedder without
  symbols calls nothing. It is how =SvgWriter::symbols= works (§ SVG
  output). One atlas a renderer: two maps sharing a GL context share one
  =Symbols=. =GpuSymbols= (built in step 1, unreleased) becomes the
  renderer's own. Ruled out: a =&GpuSymbols= argument on every draw, which
  grows four signatures and makes an embedder without symbols upload an
  empty atlas to pass; and a palette uploaded with its atlas, which would
  hold copies of the atlas's GL names, so a palette outliving its atlas
  could sample whatever texture GL gave those names next.

Ruled out:
- *Symbols in the palette itself.* A class would carry its own pixels, so
  one icon used by twenty classes would be uploaded twenty times, and a
  restyle would upload images.
- *A texture per symbol.* A draw would bind one per class, splitting every
  layer's draw call by class.
- *Signed distance fields*, for now. They scale crisply and suit masks, but
  colour symbols can't use them, and a mask drawn no larger than its pixels
  looks the same either way. They could be a third kind later.

*** Markers
- *A marker program*, compiled the four ways as the others are: an
  instanced quad per point, sized from =point_size= and the symbol's
  shape, placed by its anchor and rotated. The fragment shader samples the
  atlas inside the symbol's rectangle, clamped so a mipmap can't reach its
  neighbour.
- *Points with a marker and points without* are told apart in the shader
  by the class's marker row: a class with no marker keeps today's dot
  path, so a layer mixing dots and markers is still one draw.
- *Picking follows the image*: a pick fragment is kept where the sampled
  alpha is at least 0.5, the middle of an antialiased edge, so a click on
  the transparent corner of a pin picks what lies under it. Through a state, as =Picker::draw_state= does
  now.
- *Tile edges*: a marker drawn by one tile is cut at that tile's edge, and
  the neighbour draws the rest from its own copy of the point. So a marker
  is whole only when its reach is within the guard band and the
  neighbour's source holds the point too. Nazca checks the first: a
  palette whose markers reach past the guard band is an error naming the
  class and the size, from a check the embedder calls (below). The reach is measured from the anchor, not the
  centre, and at the marker's worst rotation: a pin anchored at its tip
  reaches its whole height above the point. Tile edges are the map's, so a
  marker rotated against the map keeps one rotation against them, and is
  checked along them at it; a marker upright on the screen turns against
  them as the camera turns, and reaches as far as its anchor's farthest
  corner in every direction (half the diagonal, about 0.71 of the size, for
  a square anchored at its centre). (The proposal had the two the other way
  round; corrected when built.) The second is the embedder's, as it is
  for lines now, and the README says so.
- *The check is against half the guard band.* Decided 2026-11-21 (user
  decision). The guard band is in the tile's own pixels, and the flat
  selector shows a level's tiles from their own size down to half of it
  just before the next coarser level takes over (=level_for_resolution=),
  while a marker's size is in screen pixels and doesn't shrink. So a
  32-pixel guard band is 16 on screen over half the zoom range, and a
  marker reaching further is cut at some zooms. Checked against half, a
  marker is whole at every zoom. With the default =guard_px= of 32 that
  allows a marker upright and centred up to 32 pixels, one turning with the
  map up to 22, and a pin anchored at its tip 16 tall; the error names the
  class, its size and the =guard_px= it needs, and an embedder wanting
  larger markers raises =guard_px= in its own =Detail=. The default stays,
  so a map without markers keeps no more geometry. Ruled out: raising the
  default, which costs every embedder clipped geometry past each tile's
  edge, unmeasured; and checking against the whole guard band, which cuts
  markers over half the zoom range.
- *The embedder calls the check.* Decided 2026-11-21 (user decision).
  =Palette::check_reach(&Symbols, guard_px)= returns the error, standard
  library only, as =Palette::blurred= answers its question: it is the one
  place that sees the palette's sizes, the symbols' shapes and anchors, and
  the guard band together. An embedder that doesn't call it gets cut
  markers, not an error; the spike and the README call it. Ruled out:
  checking at palette upload, which can't check a palette uploaded before
  the symbols it names, needs the smallest =guard_px= of every layer the
  palette styles, and gives every embedder's =update_palette= a =Result=.
- *No decluttering.* Markers overlap where their points do, drawn in
  feature order. Hiding markers that collide is the placement problem of
  plans/rendering.org § LABELS and belongs with it.

*** Dashes
- *A mesh carries distance along its lines.* =Mesh= gains =distances=: a
  =f32= per vertex, the distance from the start of its line or ring in tile
  units, measured along the snapped and decimated line *before* clipping,
  so the phase follows the geometry the embedder handed in rather than
  restarting at nazca's own clip. =Mesh= becomes =#[non_exhaustive]= with
  it, as only =MeshBuilder= builds one. A segment instance carries its
  start's distance (=SEGMENT_BYTES= 24 to 28). The shader gets the rest
  from the segment's length.
- *=MeshBuilder::set_line_start(distance)=*, in map units, before a
  feature: where its first vertex lies along the whole line. An embedder
  whose tiles hold pieces of longer lines passes each piece's offset, and
  dashes run on across tile edges without a seam. Without it each piece
  starts at 0.
- *=LINE_FS= dashes in pixels.* From the segment's start distance, the
  fragment's position along the segment and the zoom, it finds the
  distance to the nearest /on/ interval of the pattern and keeps what lies
  within half the width of it. So dash ends are round, matching the round
  ends and joins lines already have, and an /on/ length of 0 is a dot: a
  dotted line is a dash pattern. Joins stay round where the pattern is on.
  Each dash shows /on/ plus the width long and each gap /off/ less it,
  as in SVG with round caps: a gap narrower than its line closes. The
  README says so, since it is the first surprise a pattern meets.
- *Dashes keep their size, not their place, as the map zooms.* Their
  lengths are pixels and their phase is a distance along the line, so a
  long line gains dashes as the camera zooms in, and they flow out from its
  start. Keeping both would need dashes in map units, which grow and shrink
  with the zoom.
- *Picking ignores dashes*: a click in a gap still picks the line. A gap
  is a few pixels, and a line that can't be clicked between its dashes is
  harder to use than one that can.

*** Pattern fills
- *=FILL_FS= samples a pattern* where the class has one: the repeat is
  anchored to the map and sized in pixels, so it doesn't swim as the map
  pans and keeps its size at every zoom. The anchor is the grid's origin,
  taken off each tile's origin in =f64= on the CPU and passed as a uniform
  with the tile's matrix, so the repeat has no seam between tiles and keeps
  its precision at deep zooms.
- *The wrap has no seam either*: the shader picks the mipmap from the
  unwrapped coordinate (=textureGrad=), since the wrapped one jumps at
  each repeat edge and would pick the smallest mipmap along it.
- *Picking ignores the pattern*: a fill picks wherever it covers, as a
  fill that draws nothing picks now.

*** Later: vector symbols
Pixels serve any symbol, but they are fixed at the size they were drawn at:
a marker drawn larger, at a deep zoom or on a large print, blurs until the
embedder draws it again. A marker is usually a few shapes in a few colours
(a pin in the class's colour, a white glyph on it, a dark halo under it),
and a vector form of that stays crisp at any size and needs no rasterizer
of the embedder's.
It also lets an embedder build symbols from data rather than images.

- *A third kind, /vector/*: a box (as an SVG viewBox, y down), an anchor in
  it, and an ordered list of *parts*, drawn first to last. A part is a shape
  and a paint.
- *A part's shape* comes one of two ways:
  - /Path data/ (an SVG =d= attribute) with a fill rule. Nazca parses it with
    a small hand-written parser (absolute and relative =M L H V C S Q T A Z=,
    arcs converted to cubics) and needs no dependency. Only path data: no
    =<g>=, transforms, styles, gradients or text, so markup that needs those
    stays a pixel symbol. This is what icon sets publish.
  - /Geometry through the =Sink= trait/, in box coordinates: polygons and
    lines fed as tiles are, so WKB goes through nazca's own reader. This is
    how an embedder builds a symbol from data, curves already flattened.
- *A part's paint*: a fill and a stroke, each none, a literal colour, or a
  /slot/: the class's =fill= or =line= colour, read from the palette at draw
  time. A stroke has a width in box units. So one pin serves every class's
  colour while its glyph stays white, and restyling a class recolours its
  markers with no symbol prepared again.
- *Drawn with what nazca already has*: fills triangulated by =earcut= under
  the fill rule, strokes as segments in the line program, round ended. A
  stroke-based icon set (lines of one width with round ends) maps onto the
  line program as it stands.
- *Prepared once*, not per feature: curves are flattened to a tolerance at
  the largest size a palette draws the symbol at, and prepared again only if
  a palette draws it larger. The prepared symbol is instanced at each point,
  so a layer of a thousand pins prepares one pin. Its literal colours travel
  with its vertices; slots are read from the class.
- *Thinned to the size drawn.* Both kinds of part go through =MeshBuilder='s
  snapping to the pixel grid and its dropping of points that share a pixel,
  at that largest size, as tiles do. So geometry written densely, to print
  large, costs a small marker on screen only the vertices its size shows.
- *Print quality.* SVG output writes path data as it was handed in, curves
  and all, so it is exact at any size. It writes geometry with every vertex
  it was handed, unthinned, so a geometry part is as smooth on paper as it
  was written: a curve's segments have to be short enough for the largest
  size it prints at. For a circle of radius /r/ in /n/ segments the gap
  between polygon and circle is /r/ (1 - cos(π / /n/)); facets show from
  about 0.1 pixels at the output's resolution.
- *=Symbol::facets(size_px)=* says where that fails, standard-library only:
  each geometry part whose segments, where two of them turn by a small angle
  θ (under 30°; sharper is a corner) and so stand for a curve, miss it by
  more than 0.1 pixels at that size, the gap taken as /L/ θ / 8 for
  segments of length /L/. An embedder calls it with the size and resolution
  of its largest output, to warn an author before a print shows the facets.
- *Picking follows the shapes*: a part picks where it draws, so a click
  between a pin's parts picks what lies under it.
- *Limits the embedder can rely on*: parts per symbol and vertices per part
  after flattening are capped, each an error naming the symbol and the part,
  so a symbol built from data can't grow without bound. The caps are to
  measure (§ CHECKS).
- *SVG output writes each part as a =<path>=*: path data as it is handed
  in, geometry as its rings and lines, in a =<symbol>= per symbol and class
  used, with the class's colours in its slots. A =<use>= can't recolour two
  slots of a shared =<symbol>= in every viewer, and a layer has few classes.
  Exact, and safe by construction, since nothing but shapes was accepted.
- *Edges need antialiasing.* =FILL_FS= gives a fill no antialiased edge
  of its own: polygons rely on their outline, or on a multisampled
  framebuffer if the embedder makes one. An icon's thin outline at 20 to 30
  pixels, a pixel wide or less, would look jagged without either. Nazca
  can't count on multisampling: a browser embedder turned it off after it
  made panning and zooming visibly slower in Chromium on macOS, likely
  because a canvas in device pixels holds four samples of each, resolved
  every frame. So a vector symbol smooths its own edges, one of two ways
  (§ OPEN QUESTIONS 7):
  - /A feathered outline/: the fill's triangles plus a one-pixel
    antialiased outline of the same rings in the line program, as polygon
    outlines are drawn now. Drawn across the edge, its inner half lies over
    the fill, so a translucent part blends twice there and shows a darker
    rim, and every shape grows by half a pixel. Drawn only outside the fill,
    through the stencil, it has neither. The same outline could later
    smooth a plain fill with no line over it.
  - /Rasterized into the atlas/: nazca draws the symbol at the size a class
    draws it, with a small area-coverage rasterizer (standard library
    only), and draws it as a pixel marker. Its coverage is exact and the
    cheapest to draw, but its slots' colours are baked in, so a restyle
    uploads images, and a =point_size= on a zoom curve rasterizes again as
    the camera zooms unless sizes are bucketed.

Not in the first release: the pixel route draws these symbols already (an
embedder rasterizes them, or uses =Symbol::from_svg=), and this kind is
worth its parser and instancing once a symbol has to stay sharp past the
size it was drawn at, or is built from data.

*** SVG output
- *=SvgWriter::symbols(&Symbols)=* before the layers that use them. Each
  symbol used is written once, in =<defs>=: as its SVG markup in a
  =<symbol>= when it has some, or else as its pixels in an =<image>= with a
  PNG data URL (written by a small encoder in the module: stored, not
  compressed, so it needs no dependency).
- *Markers* are =<use>= elements with the symbol's position, size and
  rotation. A mask marker is the class's colour through a =<mask>= of the
  symbol (=mask-type: alpha=). Built: a mask's PNG is written white with
  its alpha, so a viewer that reads masks by luminance, without
  =mask-type=, sees the same mask.
- *Patterns* are a =<pattern>= per class, in user space, with the same
  anchor and repeat as on screen, holding a =<use>= of the symbol, tinted
  the same way. The class's CSS fill names it.
- *Dashes* are =stroke-dasharray= and =stroke-dashoffset=. SVG restarts a
  dash pattern at each subpath, so a chain whose line starts part-way
  through the pattern is a =<path>= of its own with its own offset, and only
  chains that start at the pattern's start share the class's one path. The
  cost: where two such paths meet, a translucent dashed line blends twice,
  which one path per class exists to avoid.
- *Markup is written as it is handed in.* Whether it is safe to put in a
  document is the embedder's question, as nazca can't know where the
  document goes. § OPEN QUESTIONS 1 has an option that answers it.

Ruled out:
- *Markers as =<image>= only*, from the atlas's pixels. Every marker in a
  vector layout would be a bitmap, which is what SVG output exists to avoid.
- *Rasterizing patterns into the document.* Same reason.

*** Not in this plan
- *Symbols along a line*: arrows on a one-way street, ticks on a railway.
  It needs placement along the line, its own problem; a dash pattern over a
  second, wider line row covers ticks for now.
- *An image stretched along a line* (a line drawn by a texture).
- *A rotation per feature.* Nazca holds no attributes. An embedder rotates
  by class (eight or thirty-six directions, say), and an exact angle per
  feature would need a value per feature on the GPU, which is a plan of its
  own.
- *Labels and decluttering* (plans/rendering.org § LABELS).

** OPEN QUESTIONS
1. *Where SVG becomes pixels.* Decided 2026-11-19 (user decision): A, with
   B as an optional feature over it, both in 0.3.0.
   - A. The embedder hands in pixels, and optionally markup. The default
     build stays standard-library only. In a browser the embedder has an SVG
     renderer at hand (an =<img>= and a canvas); natively it needs its own.
   - B. A =symbols= feature (=resvg=, =usvg=, =tiny-skia=; pure Rust, builds
     for =wasm32-unknown-unknown=, its font support off) adds
     =Symbol::from_svg(markup, size, scale)=. Pixels and markup then come
     from one source and can't disagree, a native embedder needs nothing of
     its own, and the markup the SVG writer gets back is =usvg='s rewrite of
     the parsed tree: no scripts, no external references, so it answers the
     safety question too. =tiny-skia= is already the planned =raster=
     feature's dependency (project.org § DEPENDENCY POLICY).
2. *Mask and colour kinds.* Decided 2026-11-19 (user decision): both. Masks
   give one symbol many colours through the palette; colour symbols are
   what most icon sets are.
3. *Dash lengths in pixels or in line widths.* Decided 2026-11-19 (user
   decision): pixels, as widths and point sizes are, and as SVG takes them.
   Either unit draws a dash the same at every zoom while its line's width
   holds; they differ only when the width changes, between classes or along
   an embedder's zoom curve. In widths (MapLibre's choice) a pattern keeps
   its proportions as its line thickens, which an embedder can do itself by
   multiplying when it writes the palette.
4. *Line distances always, or only when asked.* Decided 2026-11-19 (user
   decision): always. They cost a =f32= per vertex and per segment, about a
   sixth of a line mesh, but a mesh doesn't know its styles, and a dash
   added later would otherwise mean preparing every tile again. To measure
   in step 3; an opt-out could follow without breaking anything, where
   making them opt-in later would change how every embedder prepares.
5. *What =point_size= means for a marker.* Decided 2026-11-19 (user
   decision): its longest side, so a marker fits the same box whatever its
   shape. The alternative, its height, suits pins and labels but makes a
   wide marker larger than its size says. The two differ only for a symbol
   wider than it is tall. Decided with it: a =Symbol= carries a scale, not
   a size of its own, so its size comes from the style alone.
6. *Version.* Decided 2026-11-19 (user decision): 0.3.0, with =Style=,
   =Marker=, =Dash=, =Pattern= and =Mesh= =#[non_exhaustive]=. =Style= and
   =Mesh= gain public fields, which breaks every struct literal (the
   README's among them) and makes the release 0.3.0 whatever else is
   chosen. With plain fields every later =Style= field would break them
   again, so a style feature couldn't ship in a patch as state picking did
   in 0.2.1, and 1.0 would need the switch anyway. Made now, the break
   happens once. Fields stay public, readable and writable; only literals
   and exhaustive patterns go, so an embedder builds from =default()= or
   =new= and assigns. Two exceptions, where fields must agree: a =Dash='s
   lengths are read through =Dash::lengths=, so its count can't disagree
   with them, and a =Symbol='s size and pixels through accessors, checked
   by =Symbol::new=. Built, clippy's =field_reassign_with_default= doesn't
   fire on that pattern in an embedder's crate.
7. *Antialiasing vector symbols* (§ Later: vector symbols).
   Narrowed 2026-11-19 (user decision): not multisampling, which smooths
   every edge at once but costs memory and fill rate on every frame, was
   measured too slow in a browser embedder, and is the embedder's
   framebuffer, not nazca's, to choose. Between a feathered outline drawn
   outside the fill and rasterizing into the atlas, open until step 7's
   spike measures both (§ CHECKS).

** STEPS
0. *Nazca's own GL context* (plans/gl-context.org § STEP 1), first, so the
   =gl= code below takes =&gl::Context= from the start. It breaks every =gl=
   signature, and 0.3.0 breaks anyway (§ OPEN QUESTIONS 6), so the two
   breaks ship as one.
1. *Symbols and the palette.* The =symbol= module (=Symbol=, =Symbols=,
   shelf packing, =SymbolId=), =Style='s three fields, =#[non_exhaustive]=
   on =Style= and the new types, the 256 x 7 palette, =GpuSymbols=. Nothing
   draws them yet; existing draws are unchanged. Built 2026-11-21. Docs: =style.rs= and
   =symbol.rs= module docs, plans/rendering.org § STYLING.
2. *Markers*: the marker program and its four forms, picking by alpha, the
   guard band check, =<use>= and =<mask>= in SVG. Docs: =gl.rs= and
   =svg.rs= module docs. Built 2026-11-21 but for SVG markup, with the
   renderer holding the atlas (=Renderer::set_symbols=),
   =Palette::check_reach=, and points written once in SVG.
3. *Dashes*: =Mesh::distances= and =set_line_start=, the segment instance,
   =LINE_FS='s dash test, =stroke-dasharray= in SVG.
4. *Pattern fills*: the anchor uniform, =FILL_FS='s pattern sample,
   =<pattern>= in SVG.
5. *=Symbol::from_svg=* behind the =symbols= feature (§ OPEN QUESTIONS 1).
6. *The README* (a section on symbols: kinds, sizes and the quarter floor
   on drawing them small, the guard band rule, how round ends lengthen
   dashes, what SVG output writes; its palette
   example built from =Style::default()= by assignment; its setup building
   a =gl::Context= in place of a =glow= one) and *a release*, 0.3.0 as
   § OPEN QUESTIONS 6 says, its notes covering both breaks.
7. *Later, vector symbols*: parts from path data (the parser and
   flattening) and from a =Sink=, literal and slot paint, the instanced
   symbol mesh, picking by shape, the caps, the antialiased edge as § OPEN
   QUESTIONS 7 settles, =<symbol>= output per class. A release of its own.

** CHECKS
- *Packing*: unit tests in =symbol.rs=. Ids stay stable across adds and a
  repack; rectangles never overlap, keep their padding of 4 and start on a
  multiple of 4; an image larger than the atlas is an error naming it, and
  an add past the last id is an error too. The atlas holds each pixel
  premultiplied and the =Symbol= its bytes as handed in.
- *The spike*, natively and in Chrome's WebGL2, a key and flag for each:
  - /Markers/ (run 2026-11-21, natively and in Chrome's WebGL2;
    =--check-markers=, K in the browser, M to cycle): the county centroids
    as a colour marker and as a mask in
    three classes' colours, rotated against the map and against the screen
    while the camera turns. A marker over a tile edge is whole at every
    zoom, and so is a pin anchored at its tip and turning with the map; a
    palette whose pin reaches past the guard band only when turned is
    refused. A click on its transparent corner picks the county under it.
    A marker packed beside one of a contrasting colour, drawn at a quarter
    of its pixels, shows no rim of its neighbour's colour, and a white
    colour marker on transparent pixels stored black shows no grey rim at
    that size or at full size between pixels.
  - /Dashes/: county outlines dashed and dotted. The pattern runs on
    across tile edges when the spike passes line starts, and restarts
    without them; it doesn't crawl as the map pans.
  - /A far line start/: an =f32= distance loses the phase by about the
    distance in pixels times 6 x 10^-8 at each vertex: 0.2 pixels for a
    piece 1,000 km along its line at z18, 4 pixels for 5,000 km at z20. A
    long line with a large start, at the grid's deepest level, shows
    whether the jump is seen; if it is, the start travels as two =f32=.
  - /Patterns/: a hatch over a flat fill (two palettes), anchored to the map
    while panning, with no seam at tile edges or at the repeat, at z2 and
    z14.
- *SVG*: unit tests in =svg.rs= for each kind: one =<defs>= entry per symbol
  used, =<use>= placement and rotation, a mask's colour, a =<pattern>='s
  anchor and repeat, a dashed chain's offset. The spike's =--svg= and
  =--screenshot= of the same view show the same markers, dashes and hatch.
- *Mask support* in the viewers SVG output is read by: browsers, and a
  rasterizer such as =resvg=. =mask-type: alpha= is the part to check.
  Checked 2026-11-21 in WebKit (macOS Quick Look): the spike's pins match
  its screenshot. =resvg= is not checked yet; the white PNG makes a
  luminance mask agree anyway.
- *Points in two guard bands.* Nothing clips the SVG writer's output by
  tile, so a point held by two tiles may be written twice. Invisible for an
  opaque dot; a translucent marker would show it. Check, and if so, write a
  point only from the tile whose own area holds it. Checked when built: it
  was written twice, and is now written by the tile whose own area holds
  it, half open so a point on an edge has one tile. The cost: a point just
  outside every tile drawn, whose marker reaches onto the page, is lost at
  the page's edge.
- *Vector symbols* (step 7): unit tests of the parser against every
  command, absolute and relative, and arcs; a single-path pin from a
  public-domain icon set (a filled outer contour with a hole, cubic curves)
  drawn at 16, 24, 64 and 256 pixels beside the same pin as pixels, sharp at
  every size, its hole open under both fill rules it is wound for; its SVG
  output the path data unchanged. A pin of three parts fed as WKB (a halo,
  a body in the class's fill slot, a white dot) in three classes' colours,
  recoloured by a palette rewrite alone; a click between its parts picks
  what lies under it. The caps measured against a few public icon sets,
  flattened at 256 pixels. Thinning: a disc of 256 segments drawn at 24
  pixels prepares only the vertices a coarser disc of the same pixels
  would, and looks the same; its SVG output keeps all 256. =facets=: a disc
  of radius 30 pixels in 32 segments is named (a gap of about 0.14), and in
  128 is not (under 0.01). Antialiasing (§ OPEN QUESTIONS 7): the pin at 16
  and 24 pixels with a translucent part, feathered outside its fill and
  rasterized into the atlas, natively and in Chrome's and Safari's WebGL2
  with multisampling off: its edges against the pixel pin, no rim on the
  translucent part, the frame time of the 2,500-point grid as pins, and the
  time a zoom curve on =point_size= costs the atlas route.
- *Cost*: a frame of the 2,500-point grid as dots and as markers, and the
  mesh bytes and prepare time of a large line layer with and without
  distances (§ OPEN QUESTIONS 4).
